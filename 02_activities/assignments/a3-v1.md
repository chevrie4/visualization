What software did you use to create your data visualization?

Python with Pandas and Seaborn libraries

Who is your intended audience?

Public Health professionals/epidemiologists
    
What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 

Differences in outbreaks durations across different healthcare institutions in the City of Toronto in 2024, as well as pathogen types. These insights might inform resource allocation of PPE, Infection Prevention & Control education, vaccine distribution, and aid in decisions about discharges from acute care to various types of non-acute healthcare settings.

What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 

I tried to make a simple scatterplot that employs Gestalt principles such as proximity (e.g. clustering of data around a label) and similarity (e.g. colour) to draw the audience's attention to trends in the dataset (e.g. lots of respiratory pathogens driving outbreaks compared to Enteric, across all healthcare settings). I also tried to minimize cognitive load by only splicing the data points across two domains (Outbreak Setting and Type of Outbreak), though it's possible to divide up into specific pathogens, as seen further down in my Jupyter notebook (assignment_3.ipynb).

How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization?

All code/data is publicly available so can be replicated by others. E.g. dataset available through the City of Toronto website (https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/outbreaks-in-toronto-healthcare-institutions/), code for the visualization and associated libraries is available on my GitHub repo.

How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?

I used viridis colour palette, which makes the plot easier to read for those with colourblindness. Also the axes and legend font size is sufficiently large, and the font is sans serif.

Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?

Patients in hospitals and residents of congregate living centres, as well as their families might be impacted by this visualization, as outbreaks affect their health and safety. Similarly, frontline staff working in these settings could be impacted.

How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization?

The dataset I chose contains mostly categorical data, and I wanted to create a visualization with a numeric variable so I calculated outbreak duration based on start and end dates, which I believe can be used as a proxy for a multitude of other factors driving or mitigating outbreaks. I decided to focus on this variable across different healthcare settings, grouped by pathogen type.

What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?

Deciding on which variables to include took some time, and also figuring out which Seaborn plot would be most informative required a fair amount of experimentation before deciding on the strip plot.
